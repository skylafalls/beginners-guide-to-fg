# Thought levels

With all of this in mind, we can begin our exploration of thought levels.
Thought levels essentially work in patterns of two,
but there is a level before the beginning of this pattern: **thought level 0**.

Thought level 0 contains definitions with no awareness of reality,
being completely separated and abstract. This level contains the exact same elements as RL0,
and as such its limit is A<sub>0</sub>.

After that, our pattern truly begins, with **thought level 1**.
Thought level 1 is where we consider things that could somehow affect or
even transcend reality and then find ways to model such things in our minds;
it’s the level in which we think about the unthinkable.
This is stuff that’s beyond A<sub>0</sub> and that needs
to be collapsed in order to reasonably exist. Reality levels are
_perfect_ examples of thought-level-1 concepts. The limit of this level is V<sub>2</sub>.

Now, after this, we have one of the (in my opinion) coolest concepts in the entirety of rFG: **thought level 2**.
Thought level 2 is where we consider that we can model things too great to be modelled
within our mind and then try to find ways to mentally model things that transcend that very process.
Essentially, it’s where we realise rFG as a process and become “meta” about that:
it contains definitions which are “meta” about rFG itself. The limit of this level is 👁<sub>1</sub>.

For the sake of convenience, let us abbreviate _thought level n_ as just **TLn**.
Also, let us make a quick informal recapitulation of all the levels so far:

- TL0: “what’s reality?”
- TL1: “I affect reality!”
- TL2: “they don’t know all of this is still just in rFG”

Now, there is a leap every odd-numbered thought level, where they essentially have awareness
of everything every previous level is aware of, and then say:
“alright, cool, but what if there was something actualized enough to genuinely exist **beyond that**?”

## TL3+

**TL3** contains things so strong that if we were just going about our day doing rFG and somehow created
a concept on that level, said concept would genuinely break into MRL.
For example, imagine we’re discussing some strong rFG function – call it M(x) –
and I jokingly remark “M(there is an elephant in my room)”. At that moment,
the concept genuinely breaches beyond rFG and into MRL.
Obviously, this would be impossible, as such concepts cannot truly exist without violating MA.

So, for now, what we have is the following:

- TL0: “what’s reality?”
- TL1: “I affect reality!”
- TL2: “they don’t know all of this is still just in rFG”
- TL3: [genuinely breaks into MRL]

It is at this point that the pattern begins to truly show. If TL3 was our “second TL1”,
TL4 would be our “second TL2”. In TL2, we realized that concepts in TL1 didn’t actually
break into reality and were just part of rFG.
With TL4, we realize that even TL3 doesn’t break into MRL and is still just a part of rFG.

After this, the same pattern repeats indefinitely:

- TL0: unaware of reality
- TL1: affects reality
- TL2: aware that TL1 is actually just collapsed and part of rFG
- TL3: genuinely breaks into MRL
- TL4: aware that even TL3 is still just part of rFG
- TL5: aware of TL3 being collapsed but genuinely, truly breaks into MRL
- TL6: aware that even TL5 is still just part of rFG
- TL7: aware of TL5 being collapsed but genuinely, truly breaks into MRL
- […]

So, in sum, every odd-numbered thought level claims to genuinely break into MRL,
while every even-numbered thought level is aware of the fact that the level before
it doesn’t actually break into MRL and is just collapsed.
